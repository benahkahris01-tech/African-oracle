Our Story

Built for African
investors, by African analysts

We believe every person on this continent deserves access to the same quality of financial analysis that institutional investors take for granted. We have been following NSE and JSE markets for over six years — and we built the tool we always wished existed.

What we do

The African Oracle is a free stock analysis and screening platform covering every company listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE) in Kenya and the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE) in South Africa — over 200 companies combined across both exchanges.

We compile and present financial data including earnings per share (EPS), price-to-earnings ratios (P/E), PEG ratios, dividend yields, net profit margins, debt-to-equity ratios, and proprietary quality scores — all updated daily — in a clean, accessible format designed for both first-time investors and experienced professionals. Every metric we display is explained in plain English on our glossary page.

Our screener lets you filter all 200+ companies simultaneously by exchange, economic moat, financial strength, earnings predictability, and sector — something that previously required expensive Bloomberg or Reuters terminals to do on African markets.

Who we are

We are a small team of independent financial data analysts based in Kenya with a deep focus on African capital markets. Our lead analyst has been actively tracking companies listed on the NSE since 2018 — following quarterly earnings, annual reports, dividend announcements, and rights issues across the full breadth of the exchange. JSE coverage was added in 2022 as we expanded our research universe to cover the continent's largest exchange.

We are not fund managers, licensed stockbrokers, or registered financial advisors. We are researchers and analysts who are passionate about making African stock market data accessible and understandable to every investor — regardless of their background or the size of their portfolio.

Our approach to analysis is grounded in the principles of fundamental investing: we care about earnings quality, margin trends, competitive moats, and balance sheet strength. We believe that good long-term investment outcomes are driven by understanding the businesses behind the share prices — not by chasing short-term price movements.

Everything published on this platform is built from publicly available information. We read company annual reports, review NSE and JSE regulatory filings, and cross-reference multiple financial databases to build and maintain our dataset. We do not have privileged access to any company information, and we do not trade on or profit from the analysis we publish.

Why we built this

African retail investors have historically been severely underserved by financial data platforms. Global tools like Morningstar, Seeking Alpha, and Simply Wall St focus overwhelmingly on US, European, and Asian markets. The handful of African-focused tools that exist are either expensive subscription services aimed at institutional investors, require account registration, or present data in formats that are inaccessible to everyday investors.

We experienced this problem personally. As individual investors trying to analyse NSE-listed companies, we found ourselves manually pulling data from annual reports, building spreadsheets, and calculating metrics by hand — work that should take minutes but was taking hours. There was no single place where you could look at Equity Group's P/E ratio, Safaricom's EPS growth trajectory, and Co-op Bank's financial strength score side by side.

The African Oracle was built to solve this. It is the tool we always wished existed — completely free, no registration required, and designed specifically for the realities of African capital markets including the unique characteristics of NSE-listed banks, JSE mining companies, East African consumer businesses, and South African REITs.

We launched publicly in early 2026 and have been continuously improving the platform based on user feedback from investors across Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, Nigeria, and the diaspora.

How our data works

Our data pipeline is designed for accuracy and freshness. It combines live market data with carefully researched historical fundamentals:

  • NSE Kenya live prices — fetched daily from the Nairobi Stock Exchange data API via RapidAPI. Prices update every morning before market open at 9:00 AM EAT.
  • JSE South Africa live prices and EPS — fetched daily via Google Finance. JSE prices are quoted in cents and converted to Rands for display.
  • Historical EPS and profit margins — sourced manually from company annual reports for FY2020 (beginning) and FY2024/2025 (most recent). This allows us to show genuine 5-year earnings trajectories rather than single point-in-time figures.
  • Dividend per share data — sourced from company annual reports and dividend announcements. Dividend yield is calculated as annual DPS divided by current live price, giving a real-time yield figure that updates with the price.
  • Quality scores — Moat, Financial Strength, and Predictability ratings are calculated using our proprietary rules-based model built on sector analysis, margin trends, earnings consistency, and debt levels. These are analytical tools, not investment recommendations.

Our fundamentals data is maintained in a live database that our analysts update directly after each company's earnings results are published. When Safaricom reports its half-year results, we update the EPS and margin figures for SCOM the same day. This means our intrinsic value estimates and quality scores always reflect the most recently reported data, not stale figures from months ago.

Data is cached for fast delivery and the website updates within seconds of any data change in our system.

Our analytical framework

Our analysis is grounded in time-tested fundamental investing principles adapted for African market realities:

Economic Moat — We assess whether a company has a durable competitive advantage that protects its profitability from competition. In African markets, network effects (Safaricom M-Pesa), regulatory moats (banking licences), cost advantages (large retail chains), and brand loyalty (East African Breweries) are the most common moat sources.

Financial Strength — We evaluate balance sheet health using debt-to-equity ratios and profit margins. Critically, we apply different standards for banking companies (which naturally carry high leverage as part of their business model) versus industrial and consumer companies. A D/E ratio of 8 is appropriate for KCB Bank but alarming for a manufacturing company.

Earnings Predictability — We look at whether a company has delivered consistent, growing earnings over five years. Commodity-exposed companies like JSE miners can deliver spectacular one-year results but terrible predictability. Consumer staples and telecommunications companies in Africa tend to deliver the most predictable earnings streams.

Intrinsic Value — We provide a simplified earnings-based fair value estimate (EPS × P/E) as a starting point for valuation analysis. We are transparent that this is a simplified model — not a discounted cash flow analysis — and should be used as a comparison tool rather than a precise target price.

What we are not

We want to be completely transparent about our limitations so that users can engage with our platform appropriately:

  • We are not licensed financial advisors — nothing on this site constitutes financial advice. Always consult a CMA-registered (Kenya) or FSCA-authorised (South Africa) financial advisor before making investment decisions.
  • We are not affiliated with the NSE, JSE, or any listed company — we are a fully independent data and analysis platform.
  • We are not a stockbroker — we do not execute trades, hold client funds, or manage portfolios. We link to regulated brokerages where you can open your own account.
  • We do not guarantee the completeness or timeliness of all data — always verify critical figures directly with official company filings before acting on them.
  • Our Buy / Watch / Avoid signals are algorithmic, not human analyst recommendations. They are based on quantitative metrics only and do not account for recent news, management changes, or qualitative factors that may materially affect a company's prospects.

Our mission

To democratise access to quality financial analysis across Africa — one stock at a time. The gap between what institutional investors and retail investors know about African capital markets is enormous. We are working to close that gap, one free tool at a time.

We believe that when more African retail investors make informed, research-based decisions, African capital markets become more efficient, more liquid, and more attractive to both domestic and international capital. Better-informed investors are good for every company that lists on the NSE and JSE.
